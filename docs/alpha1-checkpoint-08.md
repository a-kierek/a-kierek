# v1.7 Alpha 1 / checkpoint 08: HDR & Post-processing

Based on CP07. A new `js/graphics/post-processing.js` module owns the RGBA16Float scene color target, exposure history (two 1×1 textures), quarter-width/quarter-height highlight and blur textures, and the final compositing pass. Terrain, raster-wall resolve, meshes and sprites write the same floating-point target; depth and wall hits are unchanged. Terrain no longer clips highlights at 1 before compositing. The final tone mapping uses a soft highlight shoulder. Bloom is a weighted 5×5 blur of a soft-threshold bright pass. Exposure uses 64 samples and log-average scene brightness, clamped to 0.80–1.42, with gradual dark adaptation and faster bright adaptation. No readbacks. Texture allocations are recreated and old resources destroyed on resize. HUD is not affected.

F4 toggles all effects during gameplay (enabled by default). F6–F10 are unchanged. With F4 off, the HDR target is still used but only a clamped copy is presented, which may differ slightly from CP07 due to float blending. The menu uses the same copy path without exposure or bloom. Initialization checks the post shader and pipelines and falls back to the legacy direct renderer on post initialization validation failure; F4 then reports POST FX UNAVAILABLE. Console diagnostics: `window.CatroomsPostFX` (available, enabled, passes, error). No changes to gameplay, saves, assets, or displayed base version.

This is internal HDR, not HDR monitor output; existing shading is approximate and not a complete linear-light PBR pipeline. Bloom and exposure are intentionally subtle and not individually adjustable yet. No full color grading, ambient occlusion or new materials. CPU/HTTP/recorded GPU tests do not verify actual WGSL compilation, visual appearance or FPS on the user's hardware. Test F4 in bright corridors and darkness, then compare with F8 on/off and after resizing.
